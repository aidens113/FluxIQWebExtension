## What C1 now covers (each proven by a test)

- Real callbacks receive exactly the lease's copy, not the original pair. Mutating the caller's or the producer's objects during the gated authority work does not change what is invoked (builtin, native→composite, controller unit).
- A different lease copy gives `lease_mismatch`, and the original pair still runs. That covers an authority that keeps the pre-refresh lease across a target refresh.
- A producer that goes stale after the callback gives `stale`. A stale entry withdraws the node seals.
- Every branch is its own event: builtin, native callback, the composite fallthrough after an undefined native answer, and the Call Subflow helper.
- Retries: 4 attempts give 4 captures with distinct occurrences. The 4th is captured after its refresh (`selector: "fresh"`, `refreshed: true`), and each executor call gets exactly its own pair.
- These mint nothing (no authority call, no event): a missing binding, a pinned definition version, a non-executable node.
- Captured rows at input and parameter positions give `captured_records`. The authority is never asked, and the originals and dataset markers are kept.
- Cancellation:
  - Gated before the callback, with the exact reason; a late lease closes once and no unhandled rejection occurs.
  - During the final freshness check, the callback's result stands and the event is `cancelled`.
  - A frame close during the callback gives `session_closed`.
- Cleanup: each lease closes exactly once on success, throw, mismatch and close failure. Malformed, thrown, empty and invalid-reference authorities stay unknown.
- With no authority, an unentered frame or captured rows, the callback runs synchronously on the original pair, with no copy and no await.
- Ports are methodless, and a JSON copy or a foreign trace reads `unobserved`.
